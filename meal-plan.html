Personal meal plan · Week one

The Sunday Kitchen

One shopping trip, one two-hour Sunday, and seven days of food that tastes like four different cuisines — with almonds, soy, and melon kept completely out of the building.

  • Goal · sustainable weight loss
  • Style · batch-cook Sunday
  • Avoid · almonds, soy, melon
  • Budget · $60–100/wk
  • Kitchen · work from home
  • Flavors · Mediterranean, Tex-Mex, Asian, comfort

Read this before you shop

Your three hard stops

Nothing in this plan contains almonds, soy, or melon. But two of the three hide in places you would never think to check, so here is where they live.

Soy — the difficult one

Obvious: soy sauce, tamari, teriyaki, hoisin, miso, tofu, edamame, veggie burgers.

Hidden: anything labeled just "vegetable oil" (it is usually soybean), nearly all commercial mayo and bottled salad dressing, most chocolate (soy lecithin), many sandwich breads (soy flour), lots of boxed broth and bouillon, Worcestershire sauce, margarine, and canned tuna packed in "vegetable broth."

Your replacements: coconut aminos instead of soy sauce; olive or avocado oil for everything; avocado-oil mayo; dressing you shake yourself from oil, lemon, and Dijon.

Almonds

Obvious: almond milk, almond flour, marzipan, almond butter.

Hidden: most granola and "healthy" baked goods, many energy bars, and a surprising amount of pesto — plenty of brands use almonds rather than pine nuts.

A judgment call I made for you: many people allergic to one tree nut are told to avoid all of them, and I do not know whether that applies to you. So this plan uses pumpkin and sunflower seeds wherever a recipe would normally want nuts. If your allergist has cleared other nuts, walnuts and pecans slot straight back in.

Melon

Watermelon, cantaloupe, honeydew, galia.

Hidden: pre-cut fruit trays and fruit salads almost always include it, as do some smoothie blends and aguas frescas. Buy whole fruit and cut it yourself.

Always read the label anyway. Manufacturers reformulate, and I can't verify what's on the shelf in your store. If any of these allergies is severe, look for the "may contain" line too — and this plan is general food guidance, not medical advice.

The numbers

What "sustainable" actually means here

I don't know your height, weight, or activity level, so I built the week around a moderate target that works for most adults. Start here, then adjust from what the scale actually does.

1,600kcal/day target
110gprotein/day
30gfiber/day
0.5–1lb lost per week

How to adjust. Eat this way for three weeks without changing anything. If you have lost half a pound to a pound a week, you are done tuning. If the scale hasn't moved at all, take about 150 calories out — one snack, or half the rice at lunch. If you are losing faster than two pounds a week or feel exhausted, eat more; that pace isn't sustainable and it costs you muscle.

Why protein is the whole trick. Every meal below is built to land 30–40g of protein, because that is what makes a calorie deficit tolerable. High-protein meals keep you full for hours; the same calories as toast and fruit leave you hunting the kitchen at 3pm. This is the single reason this plan will work better than eating less of what you eat now.

The plate, when you're improvising. Half the plate non-starchy vegetables. A palm of protein. A cupped handful of rice, pasta, or potato. A thumb of fat — oil, feta, avocado. You can build any meal on earth this way and land in roughly the right place without weighing anything.

Keep the coffee

Coffee is free. What isn't free is the 300-calorie coffee drink — that alone can erase the whole deficit and it's the most common reason a good plan quietly stops working. Black, or with a splash of milk, and you never have to think about it again.

If you have a medical condition, take medication that interacts with diet, or are pregnant, run this past your doctor or a registered dietitian before starting.

Sunday · about 1 hour 50 minutes

The one session that carries the week

This is a real sequence — the oven and the stove overlap on purpose, so the clock times matter. Follow them in order and you'll finish with eleven containers and almost nothing left to do until next Sunday.

  1. 0:00Oven to 425°F. Rice on.Line two sheet pans. Start 2 cups dry rice in a pot (or rice cooker) — it'll be done and cooling before you need the counter.
  2. 0:05Season the chicken two waysSplit 2½ lb boneless skinless thighs into two bowls. Bowl A (Greek): olive oil, juice of a lemon, 3 grated garlic cloves, 1 tbsp oregano, salt, pepper. Bowl B (Tex-Mex): olive oil, 2 tsp cumin, 2 tsp chili powder, 1 tsp smoked paprika, lime juice, salt. Spread both on sheet pan 1, keeping the halves apart.
  3. 0:12Chop the vegetable trayTwo broccoli crowns, 2 bell peppers, 1 red onion, 1 zucchini — all in rough same-size chunks. Toss on sheet pan 2 with olive oil and salt.
  4. 0:20Both pans inChicken 22–25 min (165°F internal), vegetables 20–25 min until the edges char. Set a timer and walk away.
  5. 0:22Start the chili on the stoveBrown 1 lb ground turkey with a diced onion and 3 cloves garlic. Add 1 tbsp cumin, 1 tbsp chili powder, 2 tsp smoked paprika, then a 28 oz can crushed tomatoes, a drained can each of black and kidney beans, and 1½ cups broth. Simmer, lid off, while you do everything else.
  6. 0:35EggsEight eggs into a steamer basket or straight into an inch of boiling water, 12 minutes, then ice water. Peel them now — nobody ever peels them later.
  7. 0:45Pans out. Spread the rice.Rest the chicken. Tip the cooked rice onto a clean sheet pan in a thin layer to cool — it stops the grains clumping into a brick, and it's the actual secret to Friday's fried rice.
  8. 0:50Two sauces, five minutesGarlic-lemon yogurt: 1 cup Greek yogurt, 1 grated garlic clove, juice of half a lemon, salt, water to loosen. Ginger-scallion: 3 tbsp coconut aminos, 1 tbsp rice vinegar, 1 tbsp grated ginger, 2 sliced scallions, 1 tsp honey, 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (check that label — cheap blends are cut with soybean oil).
  9. 1:05Three jars of overnight oatsPer jar: ½ cup rolled oats, ½ cup milk, ¼ cup Greek yogurt, 1 tbsp chia, pinch of salt. Lid on, fridge. Fruit goes on in the morning, not now.
  10. 1:15Slice and portion the chickenGreek half and Tex-Mex half into separate containers. Vegetables into a third. Keep sauces in their own small jars — never poured over anything yet.
  11. 1:25Chili off the heatCool it uncovered for twenty minutes, then portion: three into the fridge, two straight into the freezer. Do the freezing today, not Thursday.
  12. 1:40The produce ten minutesWash and dry the greens (dry matters — wet greens rot). Chop cucumber and tomatoes for the week. Shred half a cabbage for slaw. All into separate containers.
  13. 1:50DoneWipe down, and don't cook again until Monday evening.

The recipes

Assembly jobs and fifteen-minute dinners

Everything below assumes Sunday is done. The lunches involve no cooking at all; the dinners are one pan each.

Lunches — no cooking, ten minutes standing up

Greek chicken bowl

5 min · no cook · 520 kcal · 42g protein

Build

  • ¾ cup cooked rice
  • 5 oz sliced Greek chicken
  • Big handful roasted vegetables
  • ½ cucumber diced, handful cherry tomatoes
  • 2 tbsp crumbled feta
  • 3 tbsp garlic-lemon yogurt sauce

Method

  1. Rice and chicken into the bowl, microwave 90 seconds with a splash of water and a plate on top.
  2. Add the cold stuff — cucumber, tomato, feta — on top of the warm. The contrast is the whole point.
  3. Spoon the yogurt sauce over at the last second, never before.

Tex-Mex chicken bowl

5 min · no cook · 540 kcal · 40g protein

Build

  • ¾ cup cooked rice, squeeze of lime, chopped cilantro
  • 5 oz sliced Tex-Mex chicken
  • ½ cup black beans, rinsed
  • 3 tbsp salsa
  • ¼ avocado, sliced
  • Shredded cabbage for crunch

Method

  1. Warm rice, chicken, and beans together for 2 minutes.
  2. Fork the lime and cilantro through the hot rice — it only takes the flavor while warm.
  3. Top with salsa, avocado, cabbage.

Chopped Mediterranean salad

8 min · no cook · 480 kcal · 38g protein

Build

  • 3 big handfuls romaine, chopped small
  • 5 oz Greek chicken, diced
  • ½ cup chickpeas, drained
  • Cucumber, tomato, red onion
  • 2 tbsp feta, 1 hard-boiled egg
  • Dressing: 1 tbsp olive oil, juice of ½ lemon, ½ tsp Dijon, salt

Method

  1. Chop everything to roughly the size of a chickpea. A chopped salad you can eat with a spoon beats a bowl of whole leaves every time.
  2. Shake the dressing in a jar, pour, toss hard.
  3. Egg quartered on top.

Ginger fried rice with egg

10 min · one pan · 470 kcal · 24g protein

Ingredients

  • 1 cup cold cooked rice (day-old is better)
  • 2 eggs, beaten
  • Handful roasted vegetables, chopped
  • 1 tsp grated ginger, 1 clove garlic
  • 2 tbsp coconut aminos
  • 1 scallion, 1 tsp toasted sesame oil

Method

  1. Hot pan, splash of oil. Scramble the eggs 30 seconds until barely set, tip onto a plate.
  2. Ginger and garlic in for 20 seconds, then the cold rice. Press it flat and leave it alone for a full minute to crisp.
  3. Vegetables and coconut aminos in, toss, return the egg, kill the heat.
  4. Scallion and sesame oil off the heat.

Tuna & white bean salad

5 min · no cook · 420 kcal · 35g protein

Ingredients

  • 1 can tuna in water or olive oil, drained (check the label — "vegetable broth" often means soy)
  • ½ can cannellini beans, rinsed
  • Cherry tomatoes, red onion, parsley
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, lemon juice, salt, pepper
  • Bed of greens, or one slice of good toast

Method

  1. Flake the tuna into a bowl, keeping some chunks.
  2. Everything else in, toss gently so the beans stay whole.
  3. Let it sit five minutes before eating — the onion softens and the beans take on the lemon.

Overnight oats

3 min · make 3 Sunday · 380 kcal · 22g protein

Per jar

  • ½ cup rolled oats
  • ½ cup milk or oat milk (not almond)
  • ¼ cup Greek yogurt
  • 1 tbsp chia seeds, pinch of salt
  • Morning: berries or sliced banana, 1 tbsp pumpkin seeds

Method

  1. Everything but the fruit into a jar, stir hard, lid, fridge.
  2. Good for four days. Fruit goes on in the morning — added Sunday it turns the whole jar to mush.
  3. The yogurt is what takes this from 12g protein to 22g. Don't skip it.

Dinners — one pan, fifteen minutes on your feet

Ginger shrimp stir-fry

12 min · one pan · 480 kcal · 38g protein

Ingredients

  • 6 oz frozen shrimp, thawed under cold water and patted very dry
  • 2 cups vegetables — bell pepper, broccoli, cabbage, whatever's left
  • 1 tbsp avocado oil
  • 3 tbsp ginger-scallion sauce from Sunday
  • ¾ cup cooked rice

Method

  1. Dry shrimp is non-negotiable — wet shrimp steams grey instead of searing.
  2. Screaming hot pan, oil, shrimp in a single layer. 90 seconds a side, then out onto a plate.
  3. Vegetables into the same pan, 3–4 minutes, still with some bite.
  4. Shrimp back in, sauce over, toss 30 seconds. Over rice.

Sheet-pan salmon & broccoli

15 min · 2 min active · 500 kcal · 40g protein

Ingredients

  • 6 oz salmon fillet
  • 1 broccoli crown, in florets
  • Olive oil, salt, pepper
  • ½ lemon, garlic powder

Method

  1. Oven 425°F. Broccoli on the pan with oil and salt, in for 8 minutes alone.
  2. Push it aside, salmon on the same pan, oil, salt, garlic powder. Back in for 9–11 minutes.
  3. Done when a fork twists the thickest part apart easily. Lemon over the top at the table.
  4. One pan, no chopping board, ninety seconds of washing up.

Chicken street tacos

8 min · 520 kcal · 38g protein

Ingredients

  • 5 oz Tex-Mex chicken from Sunday, chopped
  • 3 corn tortillas
  • Shredded cabbage, lime, cilantro, red onion
  • Salsa; ¼ avocado

Method

  1. Char the tortillas directly over a gas flame or in a dry pan, 20 seconds a side. This step is the difference between a taco and a wrap.
  2. Warm the chicken in the same pan.
  3. Build: chicken, then cabbage, salsa, avocado, cilantro, hard squeeze of lime.
  4. Three small corn tortillas run about 150 calories total, against 300 for one big flour wrap.

Garlic-tomato pasta, turkey meatballs

15 min · 580 kcal · 38g protein

Ingredients

  • 2 oz dry pasta (chickpea pasta adds ~8g protein)
  • 5 oz ground turkey, rolled into 8 small balls with salt, pepper, oregano
  • 3 cloves garlic, sliced thin
  • 1 cup crushed tomatoes
  • 2 big handfuls baby spinach
  • Olive oil, grated parmesan

Method

  1. Pasta water on first. Everything else times off it.
  2. Meatballs in an oiled pan, brown all over, 6 minutes. Out.
  3. Garlic in the same pan on low until it smells sweet — 60 seconds, don't brown it. Tomatoes in, simmer.
  4. Meatballs back, spinach on top, lid for 2 minutes to wilt.
  5. Drained pasta into the sauce, not sauce onto pasta. Splash of pasta water, parmesan, toss.

Turkey chili

Sunday batch · 5 servings · 450 kcal · 35g protein

Ingredients

  • 1 lb 93% ground turkey
  • 1 onion diced, 3 cloves garlic
  • 1 tbsp cumin, 1 tbsp chili powder, 2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 28 oz crushed tomatoes
  • 1 can black beans, 1 can kidney beans, both rinsed
  • 1½ cups broth (check the label for soy)
  • Serve with: Greek yogurt, scallion, lime

Method

  1. Brown the turkey hard — leave it undisturbed until it sticks, then scrape. Colour is flavour.
  2. Onion and garlic in, 4 minutes. Spices in for 30 seconds until fragrant, which wakes them up.
  3. Tomatoes, beans, broth. Simmer uncovered 25 minutes while you finish the rest of prep.
  4. Greek yogurt instead of sour cream on top: twice the protein, a third of the fat, and honestly nobody notices.
  5. Freeze two portions the day you make it.

Veggie scramble

6 min · one pan · 380 kcal · 24g protein

Ingredients

  • 2 eggs, beaten with a pinch of salt
  • Handful roasted vegetables from Sunday
  • 1 tsp olive oil or butter
  • 1 slice toast (check the bread for soy flour), or ½ avocado

Method

  1. Warm the vegetables in the pan first, then pour the eggs over.
  2. Low heat, and pull the eggs in from the edges with a spatula every few seconds.
  3. Off the heat while they still look slightly underdone — they carry on cooking in the pan.

Meal prep

Things that took me a while to learn

Keeping the food good

  • Season one protein two ways. Half Greek, half Tex-Mex on the same tray is the single best defence against day-four boredom, and it costs you one extra bowl.
  • Sauces live in their own jars. Grains and protein keep for five days; anything dressed keeps for one. Everything wet goes on at the moment of eating.
  • Cool the rice on a sheet pan. Hot rice in a sealed container steams itself into paste. Spread thin, cool ten minutes, then box it.
  • Reheat rice with a splash of water and a lid. It comes back genuinely fresh instead of dry.
  • Never prep avocado, dressed greens, or anything fried. They don't survive, and one slimy container is enough to make you quit the whole system.
  • Dry your greens properly. Water on leaves is what turns a tub of lettuce to slime by Wednesday.

Keeping the habit

  • Freeze on day one, not day four. Two chili portions into the freezer on Sunday means two nights off later in the month, and nothing thrown away.
  • Buy eleven matching containers that stack. Five lunches, three dinners, three breakfasts. Mismatched lids are a genuinely common reason people stop prepping.
  • Your Sunday should get faster. If it's still two hours in week three, cut a component — drop the sauces or buy pre-cut vegetables. A ninety-minute habit you keep beats a two-hour one you abandon.
  • Marinate next week's chicken while you're at it. Raw thighs in a bag with oil and spices, straight into the freezer. Next Sunday starts fifteen minutes ahead.
  • One missed day is not a failed week. Eat the takeout, and pick the plan back up at the next meal rather than the next Monday.

Week two and beyond

Keep the skeleton, change the seasoning

Don't rebuild the plan. The structure — two-way protein, roasted vegetable tray, cooked grain, one pot of something, two sauces — is what makes the week work. Swap what goes into each slot and it stays interesting for months.

  • Protein: pork tenderloin, chicken breast, white fish, or a whole roast chicken instead of the thighs.
  • Seasonings: swap Greek for za'atar or harissa; swap Tex-Mex for chipotle-lime or a jerk rub.
  • Grain: quinoa, farro, or roasted potatoes instead of rice.
  • The pot: white chicken chili, lentil soup, or a chunky minestrone in place of turkey chili.
  • Sauces: keep the yogurt base and change what's in it — dill and cucumber one week, chipotle and lime the next.
  • Vegetables: whatever is cheap that week. The tray doesn't care.

Cook one new recipe a week if you feel like it, and let the good ones join the rotation. That's how this stops being a meal plan and turns into how you cook.
